# Week 4 Model Run — September 30, 2026

This is the first completed Week 4 model run.

It is a **Wednesday snapshot**, not the final Sunday lineup. Injury news, roles, salaries, and game environments can still change before lock.

## What data went into the model

This run uses:

- public NFL performance data from Weeks 1–3,
- DraftKings salaries,
- public game totals and team totals,
- our own shrinkage rules for small-sample football statistics,
- our own fantasy-point model,
- our own ownership estimate,
- and our own statistical simulations.

No outside fantasy-point projection is used as an input.

The current research pool contains 27 offensive players across QB, RB, WR, and TE. It is a curated research pool, not yet every player on the main slate.

## Why we use shrinkage

Three games is a very small sample.

If a player scored six touchdowns in three games, simply projecting two touchdowns per game forward would overreact to a short hot streak.

The input model therefore blends Week 1–3 player rates with simple position baselines.

Volume statistics such as targets and carries receive less shrinkage.

Efficiency statistics receive more shrinkage.

Touchdowns receive the most shrinkage because they are especially volatile.

This gives us a more stable starting point while still allowing strong early-season usage to matter.

## Sensitivity analysis

The sensitivity file changes one projection input at a time by +/-10%.

This shows which assumptions are doing the most work.

Examples:

**Jaxon Smith-Njigba**

A 10% change in targets changes the projection by about 1.61 DK points in either direction. Catch rate has approximately the same effect.

This tells us his projection is highly sensitive to receiving opportunity.

**Dak Prescott**

A 10% change in pass attempts, completion rate, or yards per completion changes the projection by about 0.98 DK points.

His passing-touchdown assumption changes the projection by about 0.69 points for a 10% move.

**Dalton Schultz**

A 10% move in targets or catch rate changes the projection by about 1.08 DK points.

That makes expected receiving volume particularly important when reviewing his Week 4 projection.

The complete results are stored in `sensitivity_analysis.csv`.

## Current limitations

The most important limitations are:

1. The player pool is curated rather than a full main-slate universe.
2. DST has not yet been integrated into the completed lineup optimization.
3. Ownership is our first heuristic estimate and has not been calibrated against Week 4 actual ownership.
4. The player simulation uses a normal approximation.
5. The lineup simulation currently treats players as independent.
6. Three games of 2026 data is a very small sample, even after shrinkage.
7. Wednesday injury information can change substantially before Sunday.

These limitations are documented intentionally. A good model should make clear what it knows and what it does not know.

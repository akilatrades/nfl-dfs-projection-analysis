## Scoring reconciliation

My first chat reconstruction incorrectly credited Tennessee with four points. One sack + one interception + one recovered fumble = five points. Cleveland's 27 points allowed fall in the 21–27 band, which contributes ZERO, not minus one. Correcting that reconciles the recommended roster with the screenshot total. Kyren's lost fumble costs one point; 99 rushing yards earns no 100-yard bonus.

## What held up

Irving's 24 carries, 106 rushing yards and two touchdowns supported the workload thesis. Jameson (5/91/2) and Kraft (4/48/1) supplied useful receiving scores. Golden received 12 targets; Marks received 19 carries. Their disappointing points do not mean the opportunity assumptions were entirely wrong.

## What needs improvement

Coker's four targets and 1/9 line exposed the weakest expensive receiver choice. I had not documented his routes, target allocation or alternatives adequately. Four Packers–Lions positions concentrated the roster in a game that finished 37 against a cited 53.5 total. Correlation was intentional, but its opportunity cost was not measured.

The larger process failure was recommending a roster before completing the promised reproducible screen. Published articles and a plausible narrative were substituted for saved inputs and comparable alternatives. A profitable finish does not excuse that omission.

## Controls carried into Week 8

1. Save the candidate pool, forecasts, selection code and output before kickoff.
2. Compare simpler stacks, premium-RB opportunity cost and one-TE versus two-TE structures.
3. Stress uncertain workloads without calling analyst adjustments fitted probabilities.
4. Do not force an opposing player or sacrifice points solely for lower ownership.
5. Record official result, rank, payout and entry cost; assess decision quality separately from outcomes.
6. Do not infer a repeatable edge from one cash.

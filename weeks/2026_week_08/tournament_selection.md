## Decision

My selected tournament construction is Burrow with Chase AND Higgins, Pollard on the opposing team, three RBs total, one TE. Keep Stevenson and Irving as popular opportunity plays. The passing stack is a deliberate bet that Cincinnati's scoring concentrates through its established receivers. Pollard has an individual workload reason with Spears OUT; he is not a minimum-price receiver included solely for correlation. A Tennessee response can sustain Cincinnati volume, but Pollard scoring on the ground is not mechanically positively correlated with every Bengals receiver outcome.

## First-place scoring scenario

Burrow needs an above-baseline passing day, ideally with multiple passing TDs captured by Chase/Higgins. Each receiver must earn enough targets, yards and scoring rather than relying solely on Cincinnati winning. The risk is a run-heavy Bengals win, a short passing day, TDs spread to secondary receivers, or only one of the premium WRs producing. Cincinnati -10 / ~46.5 is a real blowout and volume concern; do not portray it as a guaranteed shootout. Three Cincinnati players plus Pollard is intentional concentration, not diversification.

Malik is the weakest WR and not a floor claim. Published role evidence: 25.8% target share and 28.1% first-read share, 60% of routes from slot last week. This supports a role beyond a speculative injury replacement; weak Miami passing output and weather can still defeat it. Strange's recent targets provide a role case but his limited red-zone work and Jacksonville weather constrain ceiling. Denver remains the budget compromise.
